Donor Eligibility

You can donate if you:

  • Are between 18 and 65 years of age
  • Weigh at least 50 kg
  • Have a haemoglobin level of at least 12.5 g/dL
  • Are in good general health on the day of donation
  • Last donated at least 3 months ago (men) or 4 months ago (women)

You should not donate if you:

  • Have a fever, cold, or infection
  • Have had a tattoo or piercing in the last 6 months
  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Have had major surgery in the last 6 months
  • Have HIV, hepatitis B/C, or certain heart conditions

Donation Guidelines

Before donating

  • Eat a healthy meal and drink plenty of water
  • Get a good night's sleep
  • Carry a valid photo ID
  • Avoid alcohol for 24 hours

After donating

  • Rest for 10–15 minutes and have a snack
  • Drink extra fluids for the next 24 hours
  • Avoid heavy exercise for the rest of the day
  • Keep the bandage on for several hours

The donation process

  1. Registration and ID check
  2. Health screening (pulse, blood pressure, haemoglobin)
  3. Donation – takes about 8–10 minutes
  4. Rest and refreshments

    Frequently asked questions

    How long does a blood donation take?

    The whole visit takes about 45–60 minutes including registration, screening and rest. The donation itself takes only 8–10 minutes.

    Is donating blood safe?

    Yes. Every donor gets a fresh, sterile, single-use needle and kit, so you cannot catch any infection by donating.

    How much blood is taken?

    One standard unit is collected, about 350–450 ml. Your body replaces the fluid within 24 hours and the red cells within a few weeks.

    How often can I donate?

    Men can donate every 3 months and women every 4 months.

    Will I feel weak afterwards?

    Most people feel fine. Rest for 10–15 minutes, have a snack, drink extra fluids and avoid heavy exercise for the rest of the day.

    Can I donate if I take medicine?

    Many common medicines are fine, but some may delay your donation. Tell the screening staff about every medicine you take and they will advise you.

    Which blood group is the universal donor?

    O− can be given to patients of any blood group, so it is always in high demand. AB+ is the universal recipient.
